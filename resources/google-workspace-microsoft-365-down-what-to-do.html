Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 Down? What Your Office Should Do

Email won't load? Check the official Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 status page first, then keep the office working until service comes back.

Published November 23, 20265-minute readPublished by Odyssey Solutions

Email won't load. Calendar is blank. Somebody at the front desk is already restarting their computer for the third time. Before anyone starts changing settings, answer one question: is the problem on your end, or is the service itself down?

When Google or Microsoft has an outage, there is nothing to fix on your computers. The best thing your office can do is confirm it, keep working another way, and wait. This guide covers how to tell, what staff should do meanwhile, and what to avoid.

The short video above shows the Microsoft 365 check in about 30 seconds. The written version below covers Google Workspace too.

Is it them or you?

Both companies publish their own status pages. Check those first, not a news site or social media post.

Google Workspace. Google's Workspace Status Dashboard shows the current status of core services such as Gmail, Google Calendar and Google Meet, plus recent outages. Click a listed issue to see more details, including an estimate of when it might be fixed. Google: Check the status of a Google Workspace service

Microsoft 365. Your admin can see the most detail in the Microsoft 365 admin center under Health, then Service health. If you can't sign in to the admin center at all, Microsoft points people to its public service status page. Microsoft Learn: How to check Microsoft 365 service health

If the status page shows an active issue for the service you're having trouble with, you have your answer. If it shows everything healthy, the problem may be on your side, so keep reading.

Quick checks on your side

These take a couple of minutes and tell you whether it's one person, your office, or everyone.

  1. Ask a coworker if their email works. If only one person is affected, it's probably that computer or that account.
  2. Open a regular website you trust, like a news site. If nothing loads, your internet is down, not Google or Microsoft.
  3. Try email on a phone using cellular data, with Wi-Fi turned off. If it works there, the issue is likely your office network.
  4. Try the web version (Gmail in a browser, or Outlook on the web) if the desktop app won't open.
  5. Write down the exact error message and the time it started.

If the whole office has no internet, that's a call to your internet provider and your IT team, not a Google or Microsoft problem. Our same-day outage recovery guide covers that case.

What staff should do meanwhile

An outage is a good time to have a short plan instead of a room full of people refreshing their inbox.

  • Pick up the phone. If patients or clients are waiting on an email reply, call them. Let the front desk know email is down so they can tell callers.
  • Use what's already on your computer. In classic Outlook with Cached Exchange Mode on, mail that already downloaded stays readable when the connection drops, and Microsoft recommends using that mode with Microsoft 365 accounts. Microsoft: Turn on Cached Exchange Mode Note that Microsoft says the new Outlook for Windows does not offer this mode.
  • Google users can prepare ahead. Gmail has an offline mode for Chrome, and on work accounts your admin has to allow it first. Google: Use Gmail offline with Google Workspace Google Docs, Sheets and Slides can also be made available offline in Chrome or Edge from Drive settings. Google Drive Help: Use Google Drive files offline Both have to be set up before the outage, not during it.
  • Keep a short printed list. Today's schedule, key phone numbers and vendor contacts on paper will get you through a few hours without email.

What not to do

Most of the damage during an outage comes from people trying to fix something that isn't broken.

  • Don't keep resetting passwords. If the service is down, a new password won't help. It can lock accounts, trip security alerts and leave you with sign-in problems after the service comes back.
  • Don't reinstall Outlook or delete your account from the app. Removing a profile can mean re-downloading a whole mailbox later.
  • Don't click "restore your mailbox" emails. Scammers love outages. During a 2024 global IT outage, CISA warned that attackers were using it for phishing and told people to only follow instructions from legitimate sources. CISA: Widespread IT Outage Due to CrowdStrike Update Google and Microsoft do not fix an outage by emailing you a sign-in link.
  • Don't install "fix" tools from search results. Wait for the status page.

After it comes back

  1. Close and reopen your email app, or refresh the browser tab.
  2. Check your Outbox or Drafts for anything you wrote during the outage, and make sure it actually sent.
  3. Look for any meeting invites or replies that may have come in late.
  4. If one person still has problems after everyone else is working, that's when to call IT.
  5. Note what slowed you down, and fix it before the next one: offline mode, a printed contact list, or a backup way to reach patients.

A few things to set up now

  • Bookmark the Google Workspace Status Dashboard and the Microsoft service status page on the front desk computer.
  • Ask your admin to sign up for outage alerts. Microsoft 365 service health can send email notifications for new incidents. Microsoft Learn: How to check Microsoft 365 service health
  • Decide who tells the office "it's them, not us," so five people aren't each troubleshooting alone.
