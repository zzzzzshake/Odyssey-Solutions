Package Delivery Text Scam: How to Tell the Text Is Fake

Got a text saying your package couldn't be delivered? Here's how to tell it's fake, what to do instead of tapping the link, and what to do if you already did.

Published November 23, 20264-minute readPublished by Odyssey Solutions

A text says your package couldn't be delivered and asks you to tap a link to update your address. It shows up on your phone between patients, during a busy week, when you probably are waiting on something. That timing is the whole trick.

Most of these texts are scams. This guide shows how to spot one, what to do instead of tapping, and what to do if someone in your office already tapped it.

The quick answer

These texts are everywhere. The FTC says fake package delivery messages, usually pretending to be from the Postal Service, were the most reported text scam of 2024. FTC Data Spotlight: Top text scams of 2024

If a text about a package has a link and you didn't sign up for text tracking with that tracking number, treat it as fake. The U.S. Postal Inspection Service says the Postal Service won't text or email you unless you first asked for updates with a tracking number, and those messages won't contain a link. U.S. Postal Inspection Service: Smishing, package tracking text scams

Signs the text is fake

  • It has a link. Real Postal Service tracking texts you asked for don't. USPIS
  • You never asked for text updates. If you didn't sign up with a tracking number, the Postal Service has no reason to text you.
  • It comes from a regular 10-digit phone number or an email address instead of a short code.
  • It wants you to act now. "Update your address today" or "your package will be returned" is pressure, not information.
  • The web address looks almost right. Scam links use words like "redelivery," "postal" or "track" in a domain that isn't the carrier's real site.
  • The message is about a problem. Common lures are a suspended account, suspicious activity, a problem with your shipping address or a package waiting at the post office. USPS: Scams and scheme alerts

What to do instead of tapping

  1. Don't tap the link and don't reply. Replying tells the sender your number is active.
  2. Check the package yourself. Copy the tracking number from your order email and type the carrier's website into your browser, or open the carrier's app you already use. USPIS says to visit USPS.com directly if you're expecting a parcel. USPIS
  3. Report it. Forward the text to 7726 (SPAM) so your carrier can block the sender. For Postal Service lookalikes, USPIS also asks for an email to spam@uspis.gov with a screenshot that shows the sender's number and the date. USPIS
  4. Delete it.

If someone already tapped it

Tapping alone usually isn't the end of the world. What matters is what happened next.

  • They entered card or bank details: call the bank or card company right away, using the number on the back of the card. USPIS says to notify your financial institution even if you only interacted with the page and didn't press submit. USPIS
  • They entered a password: change it on the real site, and on any other account that uses the same password. Turn on two-step sign-in if it isn't on.
  • It was a work phone or they used work details: tell whoever handles IT for the office the same day, so they can watch the email account and reset anything shared.
  • They downloaded something: stop using the phone for work email until IT looks at it.

Why offices get hit

Front desks order supplies, receive deliveries and sign for packages every day, so a delivery text never looks out of place. Scammers also send these in bulk during holiday shopping season, when nearly everyone is waiting on a package. A one-line rule in the office helps: "We never tap delivery links. We check tracking from the order email."

If your team gets a lot of these on work phones, it's also worth turning on your phone's spam filtering and the "report junk" option, and making sure work email has its own phishing filters. Our guide to QR code phishing emails covers the email version of the same trick.

FAQ

Does USPS send text messages about deliveries?

Only if you asked for them. The Postal Inspection Service says USPS won't send texts or emails unless you first requested the service with a tracking number, and those messages won't contain a link.

What happens if I click a fake USPS text link?

Clicking takes you to a fake page that tries to collect your address, card number or login. If you didn't enter anything, close the page and delete the text. If you entered card or bank details, call your bank using the number on the back of your card.

Where do I report a package text scam?

Forward the text to 7726 (SPAM). For texts that pretend to be the Postal Service, you can also email spam@uspis.gov with a screenshot showing the sender's number and the date.

Is it safe to reply STOP?

No. Don't reply to a scam text at all. Replying confirms your number is active. Report it and delete it.
